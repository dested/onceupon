Decisions for you

  1. The free first story

    Rec: 5 minutes, plus a 3-minute Saturday story every week. Today it's 2 minutes, and a typical story runs about 5 (the packs say "about eight stories" for 40 minutes). The first story runs out mid-story, which is a bad first impression for a child and a parent. Cost: about 6.5¢ per install on Sonnet (0.5¢ on Haiku), so 1,000 installs a day costs $65.

  2. Quiet time stops the meter sooner

    Rec: the coach nudges after 30 s of quiet and the story ends after 60 s. Today it's 90 s and 210 s, so a child who walks away loses 3.5 paid minutes.

  3. Pricing model for 1.0

    Rec: keep the three packs and their prices. They're built, verified on the server and already in App Store Connect, and consumables are the easiest money to get through a first Kids review. Revisit a subscription or a one-time unlock in 1.1 with four weeks of real minutes per story and purchase rates. Options and numbers are in the next section.

  4. Drawing model

    Rec: switch to Haiku 5.5 only if the bench shows equal pictures; otherwise stay on Sonnet 5.5. Haiku is about 15× cheaper per minute after its larger tokenizer, which is what would make a generous free tier or an unlimited plan safe. I'll show you the side-by-side contact sheets before switching.

  6. The child's first name on shared story pages

    Rec: cut the "Who made it?" field for 1.0. It puts a child's name on a public URL.

  8. The parent consent card

Where the cost risk actually is

Paying minutes are cheap. The exposure is free devices and abuse: today our server takes whatever prompt the client sends, devices are free to create, and nothing stops spend globally.

Voice

The 1.0 ears

  • Apple SpeechAnalyzer first (iOS 26+, on device, true streaming, $0, no download in our app).
  • Apple on-device dictation as the fallback for older iPads.
  • If neither can run, the child sees a clear message and can still type. Today the app quietly falls back to cloud ears on five different paths; 1.0 removes every one.
  • Whistle (17 MB) stays out unless the kid-voice test shows it clearly beats Apple, and then only with its telemetry off.

What we don't know yet

  • No engine has ever been measured on a child's voice, ours or anyone's in your projects. The voice test scores 20–30 clips with known words per engine: word error rate and lag.
  • Interruptions (a call, Siri, AirPods) leave the mic showing "listening" while hearing nothing, and the meter keeps charging. Fixed in the native runners.
  • The 90-second coach line "Just say: The End!" plays through the speaker, gets heard by the mic, and ends the story by itself. Fixed by muting the ears while the coach talks.

A local drawing model

The ears are already local. Drawing locally means Apple's on-device Foundation Model, and today it doesn't fit: its context is about 4,000 tokens and our drawing instructions alone are 5,757. It would need a much smaller prompt or a trained adapter, and its picture quality is unknown. I'd run it as a 1.x experiment once Haiku has set the quality bar, not as part of 1.0.
